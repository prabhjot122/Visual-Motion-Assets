# Motion principles

## Connected, not merely smooth

The user rejected the Claude Design motion study because it was a sequence of ordinary animations. Easing alone does not create a motion-graphics narrative. Each transition needs a reason: a followed shape, thread, word, container, material or action carries attention into the next idea.

Combine Harvey's editorial geometry, spatial/material transitions and focal continuity with Lovio's clear product actions and readable close-ups. Their colours, marks, screen designs and footage are not LawVriksh assets to incorporate.

## Useful mechanisms

- A shape becomes a window; the eye follows the same outline as its function changes.
- A line extends into a rail; cards organise along it rather than popping in independently.
- A persistent container changes state after a clear action.
- A source highlight detaches into an explanatory fact or another composition.
- A camera move reveals context, then settles so the relevant content can be read.
- Editorial type or a material interlude bridges two ideas without repeating the same move at every section boundary.

These are a vocabulary, not a compulsory sequence. Build continuity around the current script's logic. See [reference analysis](references/README.md) for timestamped evidence.

## Rhythm and legibility

Spend time on complete results, not artificial loading. Count reading time after text is actually visible. Vary anticipation, impact, travel and settle; give the viewer somewhere to look. A connector or purposeful detail may keep moving through a hold, but do not add drift merely to avoid stillness. Readability is assessed at delivery size, not on a large editor monitor.

Align cuts and actions to actual speech, emphasis and thought boundaries. Measure the supplied voiceover. No fixed pause schedule or reading duration is universal; no pause may secretly freeze a visible person. In a voiceover-only film, the visuals own the whole frame and can advance while speech breathes. Do not add runtime or edit supplied audio beyond the current brief's authority.

## Reels and sound

V3 shows the user's successful reel direction: more considered editorial changes, product detail, captions and sound than the earlier permanent split-screen compositor. Treat its pacing as evidence to inspect, not a numerical recipe. For a new script, decide layout, captions and emphasis afresh; no influencer region is needed.

Sound accents should reinforce meaningful action and section turns without drowning speech. Music must suit the brief and have documented reuse rights; a sound/music track in an example is not a reusable license. Avoid copying the old soundtrack or hard-coding its BPM, cue count or loudness targets into every deliverable. Record and measure the targets chosen for the actual distribution brief.

## Tools

Existing editable examples use HyperFrames/GSAP with DOM/SVG and occasional Three.js. Reuse suitable techniques and assets, not a flattened screen capture. Three.js is an option when it improves the idea and stays within this visual language; the old film's 'only paper stacks' restriction belongs to that rejected/approved comparison, not all future films. A tool choice never substitutes for a good transition.

Review scenes against approved visual frames throughout, including after mobile reframing. Equal effort applies to abstract storytelling and practical product demonstrations. Do not spend so long on Case Map that Ask, drafting, Insights or another feature named by the current script becomes a flash.
