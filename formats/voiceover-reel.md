# Script + voiceover reel

Default for new assignments: no influencer or presenter footage. The visuals occupy the full intended canvas. Use supplied footage only when relevant to the story; lawyer-office bookends are optional, not a mandatory intro for every topic.

1. Read the brief and script; identify the audience, hook, main claim, supporting ideas and ending action.
2. Probe the voiceover and align actual speech. Preserve wording and audio unless edits are authorized. Text-only timing is provisional. Document any ASR uncertainty, particularly mixed Hindi/English.
3. Develop a visual narrative with abstract motion, editorial type, source documents, product details and optional footage as justified. Decide which ideas need UI versus a conceptual explanation. Do not inherit the old ten-section feature order.
4. Use editable scenes sized for the final format. A mobile crop must not enlarge unreadable desktop screenshots. Decide the caption treatment and test it against platform overlays.
5. Make transitions carry the viewer between ideas. Hold results for comprehension; avoid generic slide decks or a permanent two-panel layout without a reason.
6. Own music acquisition/extraction from authorized sources and sound design within licensing/budget authority. Background music is not a mandatory user-supplied input. See ../sound/music-and-sfx.md. Mix for intelligible speech, verify actual levels, and disclose when perceptual listening was not possible.
7. Export and check duration, dimensions, rate, audio, first/last frames, caption sync and representative transitions. Save project decisions and feedback.

The agent may decide choreography, scene count, visual metaphors and appropriate tools within the brief. It may not invent legal/product claims, replace supplied narration, purchase assets or publish without the relevant authority. Follow the autonomy level selected for this assignment rather than asking for every aesthetic choice.
